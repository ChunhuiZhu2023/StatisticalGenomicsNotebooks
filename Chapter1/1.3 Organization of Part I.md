# 1.3 Organization of Part I

  <p style="text-align: justify; text-indent: 2em;">
    The foundational principles of quantitative genetics, encompassing Mendelian genetics and the essential concepts of population genetics, are elucidated in Chapters 2 and 3. To date, the primary focus of my research in quantitative genetics has been on QTL mapping and genome-wide association studies (GWAS). It is evident that both of them are heavily dependent on linear models, linear mixed models, and statistical significance tests. Therefore, in Chapters 4 and 5, the linear models and linear mixed models are introduced, along with the relevant statistical methods. Quantitative Trait Loci (QTL) mapping is inherently dependent on the establishment of a genetic map, also referred to as a linkage map. Consequently, several chapters (chapters XX-XX) are dedicated to exploring the subject of linkage map construction from DNA markers (e.g., a vcf file) with the involvement of various population designs. Chapters XX-XX provide a comprehensive overview of the statistical methods commonly employed in QTL mapping. The remaining chapters XX-XX, concentrate on the subject of GWAS, with a particular emphasis on the linear mixed model approach.
  </p>
