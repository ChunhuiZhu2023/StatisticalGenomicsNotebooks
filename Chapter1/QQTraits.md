# 1.1 Qualitative and Quantitative Traits

  <p style="text-align:justify; text-justify:inter-ideograph;">&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The heritable traits that are observed in plants and animals typically fall into two categories: qualitative traits and quantitative traits. Qualitative traits are distributed discretely within a population, e.g., the seven Mendelian traits in peas and the tall/dwarf in rice varieties. For qualitative traits, the number of individuals in each category can be determined, thus establishing ratios between them. Mendel's seminal laws of inheritance were proposed on the basis of qualitative traits. However, it must be acknowledged that numerous qualitative traits are influenced by a multitude of genes and environmental factors, thus complicating the interpretation of causality. Despite their designation as qualitative traits, the polygenic nature of these traits hinders their study using classical Mendelian genetic tools.</p> 

  <p style="text-align:justify; text-justify:inter-ideograph;">&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Mendel defined quantitative traits that differ in a "more or less" nature. Quantitative traits are frequently influenced by multiple genes, in addition to being impacted by environmental factors. Here, the term "frequently" is indicative of an expectation of a trait that is governed by a single gene, with the continuous trait being significantly influenced by environmental factors. All traits that are distributed continuously fall under the scope of quantitative genetics.</p>
