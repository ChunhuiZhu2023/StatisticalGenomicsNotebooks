# Chapter 3 Basic Concept of Population Genetics

Population genetics is a field of genetics that studies how genes and genotype are distributed, and how the genetic composition of a population changes because of evolutionary forces. Quantitative genetics research strongly depends on the characteristics of the population being studied. In this chapter, we're going to learn about some basic ideas in population genetics.

# 3.1 Gene Frequencies and Genotype Frequencies
An autosomal locus of a diploid organism with two alleles (A and a) has three possible genotypes: AA, Aa, and aa. It is posited that $N$ is the total number of individuals sampled from the population, and $N_{AA}$, $N_{Aa}$, and $N_{Aa}$ are the number of genotype AA, Aa, and aa, respectively. The sample size $N$ is equal to $N_{AA} + N_{aa} + N_{aa}$. The genotype frequencies are defined as the proportion of individuals carrying a specific genotype in the entire population, i.e.,

$$P = N_{AA} / N$$

$$H = N_{Aa} / N$$

$$Q = N_{aa} / N$$

Given that diploid organisms possess two copies of alleles, the total number of alleles in a population is equivalent to $2N$. Thus, 

$$N_A = 2N_{AA} + N_{Aa}$$

$$N_a = 2N_{aa} + N_{Aa}$$

Gene frequencies, also known as allele frequencies, are defined as the proportion of genes that are present in the entire population, i.e.,

$$p = N_A / 2N$$

$$q = N_a / 2N$$

The true genotype and gene frequencies are theoretical values that are never known, but they can be estimated from the population. Typically, a hat is placed on the symbol(e.g. $\hat{p}$, $\hat{P}$) to denote an estimated value ; however, this hat may be omitted for the sake of convenience.
